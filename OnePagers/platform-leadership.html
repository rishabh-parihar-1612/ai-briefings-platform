Agentic Platform Leadership

The platform isn't the product, the outcome is · platform-leadership

frontiermixed confidence2026-09-10

Hook

Don't ask "what agent should we build?" Ask "what problem are we solving, and what's the simplest reliable system that solves it?"

Mental model

Six levels of thinking, run in order every time: problem, product, system, evaluation, operational, business. Collapsing straight to system thinking is the junior tell.

Decide

Use when the platform-leader framing applies
  • Being evaluated on whether an agent is the right answer, not just how to build one
  • Multiple teams are about to build overlapping AI capability independently
  • A portfolio or interview needs to demonstrate judgment, not a technology list
Skip when this is an individual-contributor build task
  • The architecture question is already decided and scoped to one team
  • No cross-team reuse, adoption, or ROI question is in scope at all

Aryaka

Pitching an internal agent platform to Aryaka leadership starts with the 20-item canvas, not a diagram: problem (NetDevOps engineers re-investigate the same tunnel-degradation pattern weekly), current workflow, why AI, why an agent specifically, risk level, and — critically — the value equation. An excellent triage agent with zero engineer adoption is zero business value, so the pitch leads with an adoption plan and a platform-reuse note: this agent's tool registry and eval harness get built once, reused by the next 3 support agents, not re-justified per model choice.

Say this

The platform is not the product — the business outcome is the product. Amazing orchestration times zero adoption is zero value, and that's the equation to check before scaling anything.

Trap

Climbing the agent-suitability ladder because the technology is available. The tell: nobody can say what a deterministic workflow with two LLM calls would have failed to do that the multi-agent version succeeds at.

Probe

An agent scores 95% task success in evaluation but the product is failing. What's missing from the eval stack?
Show answer

The system and business-outcome layers. Technical quality does not equal business value — users may not adopt it, latency or cost may be unacceptable, or the workflow itself may not matter enough. Agent-layer success is necessary but not sufficient.

Why is "extract shared infrastructure from repeated proven needs" different from building a platform up front?
Show answer

It's evidence-driven rather than agent-first: infrastructure gets built because multiple teams already needed the same capability, not because future agents might. Premature platformization builds abstractions for hypothetical future users instead of the one team asking today.

In the value equation (problem × system × eval × reliability × adoption), why does a near-zero adoption factor matter more than a weak system factor?
Show answer

Because the equation is multiplicative, not additive — excellent orchestration and evaluation multiplied by zero adoption still equals zero business value. A platform leader is graded on the whole product, not the strongest individual factor.
