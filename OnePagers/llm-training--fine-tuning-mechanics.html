SFT, LoRA, DPO, RLVR, Distillation

Chosen by the task, not by sophistication · llm-training › fine-tuning-mechanics

frontierhigh confidence2026-09-10

Hook

LoRA on attention layers only, at the standard learning rate, is a dated 2022 default. Configured correctly, LoRA matches full fine-tuning.

Mental model

Quality beats quantity in SFT — a few thousand curated examples beat a hundred thousand scraped ones. The technique (SFT/DPO/RLVR) is decided by a property of the task, not sophistication.

SFT (single right answer)DPO (subjective quality)RLVR (checkable answer)Distillation (economics)

Decide

Use when
  • A behavior genuinely can't be held by prompting, and evals prove it
  • Quality is subjective with no single correct output (DPO territory)
  • A verifiable checker already exists for the task (RLVR territory)
  • Cost or latency, not accuracy, is the binding constraint (distillation territory)
Skip when
  • No curated dataset exists yet — "we'll gather it during the project" is a red flag
  • The task has no checker and RLVR is being proposed anyway
  • The cost arithmetic doesn't clear a stated threshold for a distillation project

Aryaka

Distilling Aryaka's triage classifier: collect several thousand tickets with the frontier model's classification and stated reason, then curate — remove cases where the frontier model was wrong, checked against the eventual closed RCA. QLoRA a small open instruct model: all linear layers, about 10× the full-fine-tune learning rate, moderate batch size. Gate declared before training: hold recall on needs-investigation against a held-out set the teacher never saw — a missed investigation is the expensive error. At 2,000 tickets/month and about 1 cent per classification (~$20/month), the arithmetic doesn't clear the threshold to justify the project; it turns around 50,000 tickets/month.

Say this

All linear layers, roughly 10× learning rate, moderate batch size — the difference between LoRA as a hack and LoRA as a dependable default that matches full fine-tuning.

Trap

Configuring LoRA on attention layers only because that's what older tutorials say. The tell: it underperforms even when you raise the rank to match full fine-tuning's parameter count — the missing MLP and MoE layers are where the capacity actually lives.

Probe

You're fine-tuning for a task where quality is genuinely subjective — no single right answer exists. SFT or something else?
Show answer

DPO (or RLHF) — SFT teaches imitation of one correct output, which doesn't exist here. DPO trains on preference pairs, getting most of RLHF's benefit without a separate reward model. The hidden cost is the same as SFT: someone has to produce and curate those pairs.

A team proposes RLVR for improving customer-support response quality. What's the problem?
Show answer

RLVR needs an automatic, verifiable checker — a test that passes or fails, a schema match. "Is this support response good" has no such checker, which is why RLVR transformed math and code first and hasn't transformed subjective domains like support.

When does distillation clear the bar as a real project, versus premature optimization?
Show answer

When the arithmetic says so — current per-call cost times volume against the engineering cost of distilling and maintaining a smaller model, with a pre-declared gate on a held-out set. At low volume it rarely clears the bar; it turns in the tens of thousands of calls per month, or immediately if latency is the binding constraint.
