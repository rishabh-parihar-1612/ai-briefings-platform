Cognitive Monoculture

Standardizing on one model is a fleet risk · multi-agent-scale › cognitive-monoculture

frontierhigh confidence2026-09-10

Hook

"We standardised on the best model" is a quality decision for one system and a correlation decision for a population — and those point in opposite directions.

Mental model

A handful of models make most automated decisions and share opinions; when they fail, they fail together, on the same day, for the same reason. Markets solved this decades ago with circuit breakers — borrow it rather than reinvent it.

Decide

Use when
  • More than a handful of agents run in production sharing one model
  • A fleet-wide behavior change (model/prompt update) is about to ship
  • The organization is deciding whether to standardize on one model company-wide
Skip when
  • Only one or two agents exist — correlation risk isn't a fleet property yet
  • Model updates are already staged and monitored per-cohort
  • The system has no aggregate/fleet-level failure mode at all

Aryaka

If all 30 of Aryaka's per-engineer agents plus the shared triage agent run one model, they share one blind spot. Running the triage agent — the highest-leverage, most correlated position — on a different model from the per-engineer agents costs some average quality but buys decorrelation exactly where a correlated miss would be an industry-visible incident. If ticket volume classified needs-investigation exceeds 3x the rolling baseline in an hour, halt the fleet and page a human before diagnosing why — the flash-crash lesson applied directly, because understanding the cause takes longer than the damage does.

Say this

"We standardised on the best model" is a correlation decision for a population, not just a quality decision for one system. Borrow circuit breakers and position limits from financial markets rather than reinventing them.

Trap

Treating model-swappability (avoiding vendor lock-in) as the whole answer to fleet risk. The tell: swappability protects against a vendor; it does nothing about every agent making the same wrong decision on the same day because they share a model.

Probe

Your company is standardizing all agent deployments on one frontier model. What's the concern beyond vendor lock-in?
Show answer

Cognitive monoculture — a fleet sharing one model shares one blind spot, so failures become correlated. "Best model" is a quality decision at the level of one system and a correlation decision at the level of a population; those point in opposite directions.

What's the financial-markets precedent for correlated automated failure, and what specifically transfers?
Show answer

The 2010 flash crash led to circuit breakers and limit-up/limit-down bands that halt trading on an aggregate threshold without first diagnosing the cause. The agent equivalent: halt the fleet on an aggregate-behavior threshold before understanding why.

Why can't standard alignment techniques be applied directly to a system of 10,000 interacting agents?
Show answer

Alignment presupposes a bounded object — one model whose behavior you observe and shape. A dynamic delegation graph has no stable boundary; it's not just hard to align, it's not trivial to even know what the system is.
