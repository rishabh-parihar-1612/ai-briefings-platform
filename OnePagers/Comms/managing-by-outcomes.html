Manage Outcomes, Not Activity

Set the deliverable, stop watching the dot · managing-by-outcomes

situationalmixed confidence2026-09-13

Hook

Checking who's online is the tell that you never set a deliverable. Monitoring measures presence, not value — and it gets you nothing back.

The move

Replace the activity check with a named deliverable, owner and date. Then ask the person closest to the friction one specific question, instead of waiting for an idea to climb a hierarchy that has less context than they do.

Read the room

Use when you're substituting presence for a deliverable
  • You catch yourself checking a status dot instead of a deadline
  • You need an improvement idea and haven't asked the person closest to the work
  • A report is missing context, not effort
Skip when presence genuinely is the deliverable
  • The role is coverage — a bridge, an on-call shift, a support queue
  • A performance issue is already documented; that's a different conversation
  • The message is company-wide, not a targeted one-to-one ask

Real situation

[Scenario] Situation — an engineer goes quiet in Slack for two days mid-way through a 12-POP firmware rollout. Your instinct is to DM "you online?"

What you said — instead: "The 12-POP rollout — I need the rollback plan documented by Thursday noon, ping me when it's up for review." Separately: "Hey Arjun, what's eating the most time in the POP validation step right now?"

How it landed — the doc arrived Wednesday, a day early. Arjun's answer — re-running the same manual health check per POP — became a one-line automation that now saves roughly 40 minutes per rollout.

Say this

Here's the deliverable and the date — ping me when it's ready. And separately: what's the part of this that's actually costing you the most time?

Trap

Mistaking a visible-activity check for management. The tell: your last three messages to a report were "you around?" or "any update?" rather than a name and a date.

Why does a top-down mandate for innovation underperform asking the frontline directly?
Show answer

A business leader has authority but not shop-floor context. The person running the ticket queue or the POP checks daily sees the actual friction, so a targeted question there returns a usable answer instead of a slogan.
