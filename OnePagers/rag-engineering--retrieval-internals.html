Hook

More retrieved context often hurts — attention dilutes, so precision beats recall at generation even though recall matters most at retrieval.

Mental model

Index tuning is a knob, not a default. Chunk with document-generated context, retrieve wide, rank narrow, and check whether RAG is even the right architecture before building it.

Decide

Use when go past fusion and reranking
  • Retrieval quality plateaus after adding hybrid search and a reranker
  • The corpus is large enough that HNSW parameters materially affect recall
  • Multi-turn queries lose context ("what about the other one?") without rewriting
Skip when RAG isn't even the right shape
  • The corpus fits in context, is stable, and can just be cached
  • The question is structured (a count, a filter) — that's SQL, not search
  • The answer needs completeness, not relevance — retrieval silently drops items

Aryaka

A 30k-token SD-WAN policy handbook that changes quarterly is a cached-context case, not a RAG case — no chunking, no reindexing, no retrieval eval, and better accuracy than a pipeline built "because RAG is the default." Where RAG is right — the live runbook corpus — contextual retrieval on top of the existing structural chunker (prepending each chunk's heading path as LLM-generated context before embedding) is the next lever once fusion and reranking plateau, and ef_search gets tuned up for the on-call query path and down for bulk reindex jobs.

Say this

Bi-encoders are for recall, cross-encoders are for precision — retrieve wide, rank narrow. And I'd check whether the corpus fits in a cached context window before building a retrieval pipeline at all.

Trap

Reaching for RAG by default. The tell: the corpus is a stable 30k-token handbook that would be cheaper and more accurate cached in the prompt, with no chunking strategy or retrieval eval needed at all.

Probe

Why does passing 20 retrieved chunks to the model instead of 5 often make answers worse?
Show answer

More context dilutes attention, and "lost in the middle" effects mean material in the center of a long context is used less reliably. Precision beats recall at the generation step even though recall matters at retrieval — retrieve wide (k=30-100), rerank narrow (3-5).

Your vector index seems to be missing obviously relevant documents. What do you check before blaming the embedding model?
Show answer

ef_search — the HNSW query-time candidate-list size, tunable without reindexing. Measure recall@k against exact search on a sample first; a badly-tuned ef_search looks exactly like a bad embedding model, and teams usually never check.

When is RAG the wrong architecture even though the task clearly involves "our documents"?
Show answer

When the corpus is small and stable enough to cache directly in the prompt, when the question is structured (a count or filter — that's SQL), or when the answer needs completeness rather than relevance, since retrieval returns top-k and silently drops the rest.
